# ACME Solar Holdings Limited (ACMESOLAR)

## What it does
ACME Solar builds and runs solar and wind power plants across India and sells the electricity under long-term contracts. It also runs an in-house EPC arm that constructs the plants and is adding battery-energy-storage (BESS) projects.

## The numbers
- FY25 revenue: Rs 1,575 crore; net profit ~Rs 532 crore (Rs 5,323.7 million), up ~130.7% YoY.
- FY25 EBITDA: Rs 1,400 crore at ~89% margin (+43% YoY); Q4 FY25 revenue Rs 539 crore (+69%), PAT Rs 122 crore vs a Rs 57 crore loss a year ago.
- Portfolio reached ~6,970 MW with ~102% capacity growth during FY25.
- Segment economics: renewable portfolio EBITDA margin 88-89%; BESS ~82%; standalone EPC revenue Rs 876 crore with cash PAT Rs 198 crore (H1 FY25).
- Expense heads: depreciation and finance costs dominate (typical for IPPs); EPC costs sit in the construction arm — detailed split data not found.

## Lynch lens
Fast grower with utility-style economics — 89% EBITDA margins sound glorious but depreciation and interest take the big bites, as with all power producers. Growth rides India's renewable buildout. Asset-heavy, so free cash arrives late. P/E data not found here.

## TAM
India targets 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030 (national energy target); solar plus storage forms the bulk of the pipeline ACME addresses.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. New solar/wind capacity commissioning (~6,970 MW portfolio; ~102% growth in FY25) lifting contracted generation revenue.
2. High-margin renewable EBITDA (88-89%) and BESS (~82%) projects.
3. Finance costs and depreciation — the swing factors that decide net profit.

## Red flags
- Heavy debt typical of independent power producers; interest-rate sensitivity.
- Curtailment/offtake and tariff risks in merchant or state discom sales.
- Receivables from state utilities can drag cash flow.

## Verdict
A renewable-capacity compounder with power-utility economics — fine if debt stays tame and capacity commissions on schedule.
