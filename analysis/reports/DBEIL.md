# Deepak Builders & Engineers India Ltd (DBEIL)

## What it does
DBEIL builds government infrastructure: administrative and institutional buildings, hospitals and medical colleges, stadiums, memorials, plus flyovers, rail over/under bridges and railway-station upgrades. It wins most work through competitive government tenders, mostly in and around Punjab. It also sells leftover steel and cement.

## The numbers
- Sales: Rs 509 cr (FY24), Rs 579 cr (FY25), Rs 554 cr (FY26, down 4%).
- Net profit: Rs 60 cr (FY24), Rs 57 cr (FY25), Rs 40 cr (FY26, down 30%).
- OPM: 22% (FY24) fading to 15% (FY26). Borrowings Rs 178 cr (FY26).
- Stock P/E ~10.8, trades at 0.67x book. Working capital days ballooned to 315 (FY26). Contingent liabilities Rs 339 cr.

## Revenue streams
Construction projects (buildings, hospitals, stadiums) the main vertical; infrastructure projects (flyovers, ROB/RUB, station redevelopment) second; sale of leftover materials minor. Exact FY split — data not found on free screener.

## Expenses
Construction materials (steel, cement) and subcontracting the dominant heads (detail: data not found). FY26: interest Rs 26 cr, depreciation Rs 7 cr.

## Lynch lens
Cyclical small-cap contractor, not a growth story — sales grew just 9.5% over five years. Government dependence means slow payments ( debtor+inventory cycle 204 days FY26). Cheap at 0.67x book and ~11x earnings, but cheap for a reason. Lynch would pass: no niche moat, working capital eats cash, promoter pledged shares disclosure issues appear in filings.

## TAM
Indian construction industry ~Rs 12-15 lakh crore annually (FY25 estimates); government capex on infra rising, but crowded field with thin margins.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Execution speed and margin on government EPC tenders (OPM swung 22% to 15% FY24-FY26).
2. Order-book execution — order book (premium-gated) drives next-year revenue.
3. Interest and receivable cycle — delayed government payments squeeze profit (CFO -131 cr FY24).

## Red flags
FY26 profit -30%, sales shrinking. Negative operating cash flows two of last three years. Contingent liabilities Rs 339 cr exceed market cap-ish scale. Working capital days 315. Stock -51% in a year.

## Verdict
Struggling government contractor at a discount — a value trap candidate until cash flows and order intake turn.
