# Entero Healthcare Solutions Limited (ENTERO)

## What it does
Entero distributes medicines and healthcare products — it buys drugs from pharma companies and delivers them to pharmacies, hospitals and clinics across India. It also runs warehouse and logistics services for manufacturers, and grows by buying up smaller regional distributors.

## The numbers
- FY25 revenue from operations: ~Rs 5,096 crore (Rs 50,957.8 million), targeting 35-40% growth in FY25 (~20% organic + acquisitions); net profit ~Rs 95 crore (~1.9% net margin).
- FY26 (context): revenue ~Rs 6,591 crore (+29-30%), net income ~Rs 115 crore.
- 10 acquisitions completed in FY25 adding Rs 792 crore annualised revenue.
- Sample quarter economics: revenue Rs 1,409.65 crore vs expenses Rs 1,373.28 crore — thin-margin, high-volume distribution.
- Expense heads: cost of goods (bulk), plus distribution/freight and employee costs as the key operating drivers; interest costs from acquisition debt matter at the net level.

## Lynch lens
Turnaround-ish consolidator in a boring business — pharma distribution smells of warehouses and expiry dates, exactly what Lynch likes. The play: roll up India's fragmented drug distribution, gain scale, and let operating leverage lift the ~1.9% margin. At ~25x FY27E earnings (per one analysis) it needs flawless integration. Not yet a proven compounder — Q4 FY25 profit fell sharply YoY.

## TAM
Indian pharmaceutical distribution market — exact size data not found; a multi-lakh-crore, fragmented trade ripe for consolidation (per CompoundingAI analysis).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Operating leverage — revenue growing faster than distribution/employee costs lifts the thin margin.
2. Acquisition integration (10 deals in FY25; Rs 792 crore annualised revenue added) — synergies or indigestion.
3. Working-capital management and interest costs on acquisition debt.

## Red flags
- Razor-thin ~1.9% net margin; one bad quarter sinks optics (Q4 FY25 PAT fell sharply).
- Acquisition-led growth carries integration and goodwill risk.
- Receivables/inventory heavy; cash conversion weak by nature of distribution.

## Verdict
A scale-story in the least glamorous corner of healthcare — works if the roll-up compounds margins, not just revenue.
