# IndiQube Spaces Ltd (INDIQUBE)

## What it does
IndiQube runs managed office spaces — it leases buildings, fits them out, and rents desks, suites, and offices to companies under one managed contract. Clients get ready offices with tech and services; IndiQube earns rent and facility fees. It listed after a 2025 IPO.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 831 crore (FY24) → Rs 1,059 crore (FY25); TTM growth ~39%.
- PAT: loss of Rs 342 crore (FY24) → loss of Rs 140 crore (FY25) — narrowing.
- Borrowings: Rs 4,095 crore (FY25). ROE -42%. Market cap Rs 4,078 crore; P/E not meaningful (loss-making); trades ~7.9x book (Aug 2026).
- Expense split: lease rentals and fit-out costs dominate (exact % data not found).

## Lynch lens
Turnaround candidate. Losses shrink as seats fill and buildings season — the flex-office model turns profitable at high occupancy. But Rs 4,095 crore of debt against losses means shareholders carry real risk. Lynch hated paying for stories without profits; here you need faith in the occupancy curve.

## TAM
Indian flexible/managed office market: fast-growing post-pandemic as enterprises adopt flex space; industry size estimates around USD 2-3 billion and rising (exact figure data not found in fetched sources).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Occupancy levels across hubs — each filled seat carries high incremental margin.
2. Lease costs and finance costs on Rs 4,095 crore debt.
3. New centre ramp-up (pre-occupancy losses drag until mature).

## Red flags
- Still loss-making; ROE deeply negative.
- Heavy debt and lease obligations; rising interest rates hurt.
- Competition from WeWork-style peers and Awfis; real-estate cycle risk.

## Verdict
Speculative turnaround — losses narrow, but debt makes this a watchlist stock, not a buy, until profits arrive.
