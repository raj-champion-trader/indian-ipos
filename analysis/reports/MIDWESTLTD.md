# Midwest Ltd (MIDWESTLTD)

## What it does
Company mines and processes natural stone — dominant in Black Galaxy and Absolute Black granite. Also makes diamond wire (cutting tools), quartz surfaces, and develops heavy mineral sands (beach sand mining). Sells granite slabs and quartz in India and abroad.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 310 crore (FY23) → Rs 342 crore (FY24) → Rs 370 crore (FY25) → Rs 422 crore (FY26).
- Net profit: Rs 45 crore (FY23) → Rs 73 crore (FY24) → Rs 88 crore (FY25) → Rs 98 crore (FY26).
- OPM: 21% (FY23) → 29% (FY24) → 31% (FY25) → 34% (FY26) — steady expansion.
- Low debt: borrowings Rs 51 crore; interest Rs 8 crore (Mar 2026).
- ROE ~14.9%; tax rate ~26% (FY26); total expenses Rs 280 crore = 66% of sales.
- Market cap ~Rs 4,002 crore; P/E ~38.2 (Aug 2026). Promoters 77.1%.

## Lynch lens
Asset play with fast-grower margins. Owning granite quarries (Black Galaxy comes from one Indian belt) works like owning a scarce resource — competitors cannot replicate the deposit. OPM climbing 21%→34% over four years shows pricing power. But P/E 38 for ~11-14% sales growth gives a rich PEG (~3); margin expansion, not revenue, drives the story. High promoter ownership (77%) aligns interests; minority float small.

## TAM
Indian natural stone (granite) industry estimated at USD 5+ billion with exports a large share; engineered quartz market growing ~10%+ globally (exact sourced figure: data not found).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Granite export realisations on Black Galaxy/Absolute Black slabs (scarce-quarry pricing power).
2. Mining and processing costs — 66% of sales covers all expenses (FY26).
3. New ventures: quartz surfaces and heavy mineral sands adding future volume; depreciation Rs 19 crore (FY26).

## Red flags
- Debtor days up sharply from 50 to 143 — receivables piling up.
- P/E 38 leaves little cushion if granite prices cycle down.
- Small float, promoter-heavy register; execution risk in unproven mineral sands venture.

## Verdict
Scarce-quarry asset play with expanding margins — good business, but the market already pays a full price.
