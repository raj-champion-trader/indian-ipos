# Caliber Mining and Logistics Limited (CMLL)

## What it does
CMLL digs out coal for Coal India subsidiaries — it removes the earth above coal seams (overburden), extracts coal, loads rakes, and trucks coal to customers. It owns no mines; it works as a contract miner (MDO) with a fleet of 1,911 vehicles including tippers, excavators, and loaders, mainly in Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Chhattisgarh.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 957.92 cr (FY24), Rs 1,435.57 cr (FY25), Rs 1,684.66 cr (FY26) — 33% CAGR.
- PAT: Rs 95.90 cr (FY24), Rs 131.55 cr (FY25), Rs 157.90 cr (FY26); PAT margin ~9-10%; OPM ~25-26% (screener standalone).
- Debt-equity 1.63 (Mar 2026); borrowings Rs 754 cr, interest Rs 81 cr (FY26 screener).
- Order book Rs 9,551 cr (May 2026) — nearly 6x FY26 revenue.
- IPO (2026) priced at 17.56x FY26 P/E.

## Lynch lens
A fast grower with a boring, dusty business — contract mining for Coal India PSUs. Revenue up 33% CAGR, order book covers years of work, P/E under 18. Lynch would like the dullness and visibility. Catch: no mines of its own, so pricing power stays with the customer, and leverage (D/E 1.63) funds the fleet.

## TAM
India coal MDO (mine-developer-operator) market size: data not found in sources. Coal India's stated target of 1 billion tonnes production and rising outsourced mining gives the tailwind; order book growth (Rs 5,668 cr Mar 2026 to Rs 9,551 cr May 2026) proves demand.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Coal mining services — ~85% of FY26 revenue (OB removal, extraction for WCL/NCL).
2. Diesel and machinery operating costs — the key margin swing factor for the tipper/excavator fleet.
3. Logistics and rake loading — ~15% of FY26 revenue.

## Red flags
- Two customers (Western Coalfields, Northern Coalfields) drive the business.
- Debt-equity 1.63; contingent liabilities Rs 458.53 cr (Mar 2026).
- Zero ownership of the resource it mines — contract renewal risk.

## Verdict
Cheap, fast-growing contract coal miner with a fat order book — worth owning with the debt risk in mind.
