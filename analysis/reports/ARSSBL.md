# Anand Rathi Share & Stock Brokers (ARSSBL)

## What it does
The company runs a full-service stock brokerage. It earns broking commissions, lends money to clients for trading (margin funding), and distributes mutual funds and other investment products to retail investors, wealthy families and institutions. It belongs to the Anand Rathi group, which also spans wealth management, lending and insurance broking.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 468 crore (FY23); Rs 681 crore (FY24); Rs 844 crore (FY25)
- PAT: Rs 39 crore (FY23); Rs 79 crore (FY24); Rs 104 crore (FY25)
- OPM: 25% (FY23) → 34% (FY24) → 37% (FY25)
- Interest costs: Rs 147 crore (FY25) — the biggest single expense, funding margin book
- Depreciation Rs 25 crore (FY25); tax rate ~26%
- 3-year sales CAGR ~26%; 3-year profit growth ~50%

## Lynch lens
Fast grower riding India's retail market-participation boom — revenue and PAT compounding at 25-50% with margins expanding (25% → 37% OPM, FY23-FY25). But a cyclical twist lurks: broking volumes and margin funding shrink when markets fall. The Anand Rathi brand and HNI/family-office niche give a moat that discount brokers lack.

## TAM
India has ~19 crore demat accounts and growing (FY25, NSDL/CDSL data); broking industry revenue pool estimated in tens of thousands of crore rupees annually; margin-trading book growing double digits.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Cash market and F&O broking volumes — commission income scales with turnover
2. Margin trading book — interest spread over funding cost
3. Distribution income from mutual funds/other products — recurring, market-linked

## Red flags
- Heavy dependence on bull-market activity — profit can halve in a bear phase
- Interest cost of Rs 147 crore (FY25) is 17% of revenue — rate sensitive
- Sebi's F&O tightening rules threaten derivatives broking income
- Discount-broker price competition

## Verdict
A cyclical fast grower at peak-cycle economics — buy the dip when markets panic, not when volumes peak.
