# Ola Electric Mobility Limited (OLAELEC)

## What it does
Company makes electric scooters and motorcycles sold directly to buyers through its own website and stores, and builds battery cells and EV software in-house. It sells only in India today. Listed August 2024.

## The numbers
- FY24 revenue Rs 5,126 crore, net loss Rs 1,584 crore.
- FY25 revenue Rs 4,645 crore (~9% decline YoY) despite higher vehicle deliveries; FY25 net loss wider than FY24 (exact figure data not found in verified sources).
- FY25 total expenses Rs 6,253 crore, of which cost of materials consumed Rs 3,600 crore (58% of expenses).
- Q4 FY25 net loss ~Rs 91 crore (standalone basis per filing); Q1 FY26 consolidated net loss Rs 428 crore (vs Rs 870 crore in prior quarter).
- Warranty, employee and overhead costs make up much of the remaining expenses.

## Lynch lens
Lynch avoided story stocks with no earnings — this one fits that warning. Revenue fell while volumes rose, meaning price cuts and discounts to defend share. Category: speculative turnaround at best; losses continue, and the "asset play" (gigafactory, cells) has not yet produced profits. P/E: not meaningful (loss-making). Insider buying: data not found.

## TAM
Indian electric two-wheeler market: EVs hold a low-teens share of ~16-17 million annual two-wheeler sales, so the TAM spans millions of units as electrification rises (source: industry estimates — exact figure data not found).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Material costs (Rs 3,600 crore FY25, 58% of expenses) — battery/cell prices decide loss width.
2. Scooter volumes and pricing — discounts in FY25 shrank revenue per unit despite higher deliveries.
3. Warranty and service provisions — past quality issues keep dragging the P&L.

## Red flags
- Persistent large losses; revenue declining despite volume growth.
- Quality/warranty issues and customer-service complaints.
- Cash burn requires ongoing funding; competition from established two-wheeler makers intensifies.

## Verdict
Loss-making story stock — Lynch would watch from the sidelines until losses stop widening.
