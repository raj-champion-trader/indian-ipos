# Aegis Vopak Terminals Ltd (AEGISVOPAK)

## What it does
Owns and runs storage terminals for LPG and liquid chemicals at Indian ports — the big tanks that importers and traders rent to hold fuel and chemicals before distribution. A joint venture between Aegis Logistics (India) and Royal Vopak (Netherlands).

## The numbers
- Sales: Rs 562 cr (FY24) → Rs 789 cr (FY25), +40%; TTM Rs 904 cr
- PAT: Rs 87 cr (FY24) → Rs 225 cr (FY25), +159%; TTM Rs 286 cr
- OPM: 73-75% — toll-road-like economics
- Interest: Rs 193 cr (FY25) → Rs 110 cr (FY26); Depreciation Rs 148-208 cr
- Borrowings Rs 3,731 cr (FY26); ROCE 7.56%, ROE 11%
- Market cap ~Rs 29,905 cr; P/E ~110; P/B ~6.97; dividend payout 71% (FY26)
- 3-yr sales CAGR 38%

## Lynch lens
Asset play meets infrastructure toll-booth. Storage tanks behave like toll roads — 74% operating margins, capacity contracted long-term, and India's rising LPG/chemical imports fill them. Lynch would love the boring-tank economics but choke on the price: 110x earnings, 7x book, ROCE only 7.6% because tank capex stays heavy. Recent IPO (2025); profit CAGR of 1,472% off a tiny base flatters the record.

## TAM
India LPG import infrastructure and liquid-tank storage demand grows with energy imports; India imports roughly 60%+ of its LPG. Precise terminal-storage TAM data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Capacity additions — new tanks convert directly into revenue (74% OPM)
2. LPG/chemical import volume growth through Indian ports
3. Finance costs — Rs 3,731 cr borrowings; falling interest adds straight to PAT

## Red flags
- P/E ~110 and 7x book price in decades of growth
- ROCE 7.6% well below cost of capital on new tank capex
- Rs 3,731 cr debt (FY26)
- Related-party dependence on Aegis group volumes

## Verdict
Wonderful toll-booth business with fortress margins — but at 110x earnings, the tolls already got collected by earlier buyers.
