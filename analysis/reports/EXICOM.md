# Exicom Tele-Systems Ltd (EXICOM)

## What it does
Company does two things: it makes DC power systems that keep telecom towers and networks running (critical power), and it makes EV chargers — home AC chargers and public DC fast chargers — for car makers and fleet operators. It acquired Australian/US fast-charger maker Tritium in 2024. Listed via IPO in February 2024.

## The numbers
- FY25 consolidated: revenue ~Rs 752 crore; EBITDA ~Rs 39 crore; PAT ~Rs 21 crore.
- FY25 segment: EV chargers ~Rs 297 crore (~40%); critical power the balance (~Rs 455 crore).
- FY26: revenue ~Rs 1,152 crore (+33% YoY) but consolidated net loss ~Rs 265-274 crore, driven by Tritium integration losses.
- Q1 FY27: revenue Rs 331 crore (+61% YoY); net loss narrowed to ~Rs 69 crore; EV charger revenue Rs 154 crore (+49.6% YoY) though the segment still lost ~Rs 69 crore.

## Lynch lens
Turnaround with a fast-grower core — the India critical-power business earns steady profits; Tritium turned the consolidated P&L deep red. EV charging fits a megatrend, but losses must peak and roll over. Watch quarterly Tritium burn: loss narrowing from Rs 265 cr (FY26) to Rs 69 cr (Q1 FY27) suggests progress.

## TAM
India EV charger market — small today but growing several-fold with EV adoption; global DC fast-charging market multi-billion (exact sourced figures: data not found).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Critical power (telecom DC systems) — the profit engine.
2. India EV charger growth (home AC + public DC, +49-71% YoY).
3. Tritium (US/AU) losses — the biggest drag; turnaround decides the consolidated P&L.

## Red flags
- Large accumulated losses from Tritium; integration/execution risk abroad.
- EV charger pricing pressure and tender-based competition.
- Thin FY25 margins (EBITDA ~5%) even before Tritium drag.
- Dilution/funding risk while loss-making.

## Verdict
Turnaround bet — solid Indian cash cow buried under Tritium losses; the story works only if the overseas bleeding stops within a few quarters.
