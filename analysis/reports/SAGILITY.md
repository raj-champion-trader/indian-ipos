# Sagility India Limited (SAGILITY)

## What it does
Sagility provides back-office and technology services to US healthcare companies — insurers (payers) and hospital groups (providers). It processes claims, manages member data, and runs clinical support from India. All revenue comes from US clients. EQT-owned; listed November 2024.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 1,495 crore (FY24) → Rs 1,717 crore (FY25) → Rs 1,971 crore (FY26, standalone). 3-year sales CAGR 13%.
- PAT: Rs 73 crore (FY24) → Rs 271 crore (FY25) → Rs 351 crore (FY26). PAT jump partly reflects depreciation step-down post-IPO restructure.
- OPM: 34% (FY24) → 29-30% (FY25-26). Interest cost Rs 93 crore (FY25) → Rs 73 crore (FY26). Depreciation fell from Rs 357 crore (FY24) to Rs 99 crore (FY25).
- Borrowings: Rs 787 crore (Mar 2026), reduced. ROE only ~3-4%. Market cap Rs 21,693 crore; P/E ~59 (Aug 2026).
- Expense split: employee cost dominates (services business); exact % data not found.

## Lynch lens
Boring business, expensive stock. Claims processing for US insurers — steady, sticky, unglamorous. But ROE under 4% and P/E ~59 on 13% revenue growth makes no Lynch sense (PEG far above 1). Promoters (EQT) at 50.95% with post-IPO stake sales by promoter/anchor groups — watch for further selldown.

## TAM
US healthcare outsourcing market: Sagility holds 1.23% of it (2023, per company/screener) — implying a very large addressable pool in the tens of billions of dollars (exact size data not found in fetched sources).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Revenue growth from US payer/provider clients and new acquisitions.
2. Depreciation and interest — the FY25-26 profit jump largely tracks these falling post-IPO.
3. Employee cost/repricing and utilisation.

## Red flags
- Single-country (US) and concentrated client exposure; healthcare policy changes (US elections, payer economics) hit directly.
- Low ROE despite profits — IPO capital sits idle.
- Valuation: P/E ~59 for mid-single-digit ROE.

## Verdict
Decent boring business wearing a very expensive price tag — skip until valuation or returns on capital improve.
