# Mangal Electrical Industries Ltd (MEIL)

## What it does
Makes the metal hearts of electrical transformers — CRGO cores, cut laminations, core assemblies and amorphous cores — plus transformers (5 KVA to 10 MVA) and circuit breakers, and does substation EPC work. Trades in CRGO/CRNO coils and amorphous ribbon. Serves India's power-transmission equipment makers.

## The numbers
- Sales: Rs 449 cr (FY24) → Rs 549 cr (FY25) → Rs 580 cr (FY26)
- PAT: Rs 21 cr (FY24) → Rs 47 cr (FY25) → Rs 43 cr (FY26)
- OPM: 9% (FY24) → 15% (FY25) → 12% (FY26)
- Interest Rs 12 cr (FY26); Depreciation Rs 6 cr (FY26)
- Borrowings down from Rs 149 cr (FY25) to Rs 45 cr (FY26) — nearly debt-free
- Promoters hold 74.79% (Mar 2026)
- 5-yr sales CAGR 37%, profit CAGR 109%
- Order book figure data not found

## Lynch lens
Small fast grower riding the power-grid capex supercycle (transformers and cores in short supply globally). 37% five-year sales CAGR, debt nearly cleared, and promoter skin in the game (74.8%). The niche — CRGO core processing for transformer OEMs — sits up-stream of a supply bottleneck, which supports pricing. FY26 saw mild margin pullback (OPM 15%→12%), so watch for consistency rather than one blowout year.

## TAM
India transformer and transformer-core market estimated at Rs 30,000+ cr, boosted by transmission expansion and global transformer shortage (industry estimates; precise figure data not found).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Power transmission capex — domestic transformer demand exceeds supply
2. CRGO raw-material cost and availability (import-dependent steel) vs pass-through
3. Debt reduction — interest costs shrinking, boosting PAT from FY25 onward

## Red flags
- OPM fell from 15% (FY25) to 12% (FY26); PAT dipped too
- CRGO steel import dependence creates margin risk
- Small scale (~Rs 580 cr sales) vs large listed peers
- High promoter holding — typical of family-run firm; governance depends on them
- Order book not visible

## Verdict
Niche transformer-components fast grower on the right side of the grid capex cycle — worth tracking, valuation-dependent.
