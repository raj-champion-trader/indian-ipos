# Sanstar Ltd (SANSTAR)

## What it does
Sanstar turns maize into specialty ingredients — starches, liquid glucose, dextrose and derivatives — for food makers, animal feed and industrial users. It ranks among India's largest exporters in this maize-processing niche, with capacity of 1,100 TPD expanding to 2,100 TPD at Dhule.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 760 cr (FY23), Rs 1,110 cr (FY24), Rs 953 cr (FY25), Rs 785 cr (FY26, standalone)
- Net profit: Rs 41 cr (FY23), Rs 67 cr (FY24), Rs 44 cr (FY25), Rs 34 cr (FY26)
- OPM slid from 9% (FY24) to 4.8% (FY26) — classic agri-commodity squeeze
- Interest Rs 1 cr, depreciation Rs 10 cr (FY26); P/E ~49; ROE ~5%

## Lynch lens
A cyclical, commodity-spread business: profit depends on the gap between maize prices and starch/derivative prices, not on brand power. The Dhule expansion doubles capacity, giving an option on the next upcycle — but at P/E ~49 on trough earnings of Rs 34 cr, the market pays ahead of the recovery. No dividends despite profits.

## TAM
Indian maize-processing / starch-derivatives market — several million tonnes annually; exact figure data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Maize input cost vs finished-product price spread (the single biggest swing factor)
2. Capacity utilisation at existing plants and Dhule ramp-up
3. Export mix (India's #2 exporter) and dollar realisations

## Red flags
- Two straight years of falling revenue and profit (FY24 peak to FY26)
- OPM nearly halved to 4.8% (FY26)
- Expansion capex landing into a soft pricing cycle

## Verdict
Cyclical at a cyclical-peak valuation — wait for the spread to turn or the price to fall.
