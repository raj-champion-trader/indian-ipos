# Stanley Lifestyles Ltd (STANLEY)

## What it does
Company designs, makes, and sells luxury and super-premium furniture — mainly sofas and seating — in India through its own store network (Stanley, Sofas & More, Boutique, and Level Next formats). Vertically integrated from design to retail.

## The numbers
- Standalone revenue: Rs 228 cr (FY23), Rs 231 cr (FY24), Rs 218 cr (FY25); TTM Rs 203 cr.
- PAT: Rs 9 cr (FY23), Rs 16 cr (FY24), Rs 19 cr (FY25); TTM Rs 8 cr.
- OPM: 6% (FY23), 9% (FY24/FY25), 5% (TTM).
- Borrowings Rs 83 cr (Mar FY26); 3-yr ROE ~5%; earnings include other income of Rs 22.6 cr.

## Lynch lens
Boring niche player — luxury Indian furniture earns no analyst love, and the brand holds a defensible premium corner. But growth stalls: sales flat around Rs 220 cr for three years, TTM OPM back to 5%, and core profit (ex-other-income) looks weak. Without growth this becomes a value trap candidate rather than a fast grower. Insiders: promoter stake change data not found.

## TAM
Indian residential furniture market estimated ~Rs 1.2-1.5 lakh cr, growing high single digits (FY23 industry estimates); premium/luxury slice a small but rising share.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Sofa/seating sales through own stores — the core revenue and margin engine.
2. Other income (Rs 22.6 cr) — propping up reported PAT above core earnings.
3. Leather/foam raw material costs and store operating costs — the margin swing.

## Red flags
- Revenue shrinking (Rs 231 cr FY24 to Rs 218 cr FY25 to Rs 203 cr TTM).
- PAT quality: other income materially pads profits.
- ROE ~5%; no dividend despite profits.

## Verdict
Premium brand, shrinking sales, padded profits — wait for growth to return before paying luxury prices.
