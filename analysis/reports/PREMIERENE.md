# Premier Energies Ltd (PREMIERENE)

## What it does
Premier Energies makes solar cells and solar panels at large factories in India. It sells to solar power-plant builders and exports cells, and also runs installation (EPC) and maintenance services. It holds roughly all of India's solar cell exports to the US and ranks among the top global module makers.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 3,144 cr (FY24) → Rs 6,519 cr (FY25) → Rs 7,824 cr (FY26).
- PAT: Rs 231 cr (FY24) → Rs 937 cr (FY25) → Rs 1,510 cr (FY26) — profit multiplied ~6.5x in two years.
- OPM: 15% (FY24) → 27% (FY25) → 30% (FY26). ROCE ~33%, ROE ~42% (FY26).
- Borrowings Rs 3,707 cr (Mar 2026); interest Rs 158-165 cr well covered by PBT of ~Rs 1,973 cr (FY26).
- P/E ~27.6x (Aug 2026).

## RevenueStreams
Solar cells and modules form the dominant stream (bulk of revenue, FY26); technical advisory/EPC and O&M add the remainder. Exact split: data not found.

## Lynch lens
Fast grower riding India's solar buildout plus the US export niche (near-monopoly on cell exports to the US). ALMM duties and PLI subsidies protect domestic pricing. PEG ~0.9 on FY26 numbers (P/E 27.6 vs ~60% FY25 PAT growth, though growth now normalising) — not cheap, not silly. Insider buying: data not found.

## TAM
India solar module market projected in the tens of GW annually with multi-lakh-crore capex pipeline under the national renewable target of 500 GW by 2030 (industry estimates, mid-2020s). Precise figure: data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Solar cell + module sales at higher TOPCon margins after capacity expansion.
2. US exports protected by tariff walls on Chinese rivals.
3. Falling poly input costs boosting margins (FY25-FY26).

## Expenses
Operating costs (COGS) dominate (~70% of sales, FY26); depreciation Rs 391 cr; interest Rs 165 cr (FY26 TTM).

## Red flags
- Cyclical: Chinese module price crashes can crush global prices.
- Heavy capex and rising debt (Rs 3,707 cr).
- Policy dependence on ALMM/PLI protection.

## Verdict
A well-placed fast grower in solar manufacturing — good story, but pay attention to module price cycles before chasing.
