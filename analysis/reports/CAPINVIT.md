# Capital Infra Trust (CAPINVIT)

## What it does
Trust owns finished national highway projects and collects toll/annuity income from NHAI. It buys roads from sponsors and third parties and passes rental-style cash flows to unit holders. An infrastructure investment trust (InvIT) incorporated 2023, listed January 2025.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 1,466 cr (FY24); Rs 404 cr (FY25) — the FY24 figure reflects pre-restructuring/one-time character of a young InvIT; TTM Rs 717 cr.
- PAT: Rs 126 cr (FY24); loss Rs 77 cr (FY25); TTM Rs 537 cr.
- FY25 expenses tiny: Rs 17 cr total, interest Rs 21 cr.
- Borrowings Rs 2,363 cr (Mar 2025). AAA/Stable from CRISIL and CARE (Aug 2026). Dividend yield ~3.0% (TTM); P/E 5.3 (TTM).

## Lynch lens
Asset play / income stock. Lynch's "assets paying you to wait" category: annuity highway cash flows, AAA-rated leverage, government counterparty. Not a grower unless it keeps acquiring roads. The FY25 loss comes from InvIT structure mechanics (interest, fees, one-offs), not road performance — TTM profit of Rs 537 cr shows normalised earnings.

## TAM
Indian highways monetisation / InvIT pipeline — NHAI ToT and InvIT pipeline runs into tens of billions of dollars; specific sourced figure: data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Annuity/toll collections from NHAI road concessions — the entire revenue engine.
2. Cost of debt — Rs 2,363 cr borrowings (FY25); AAA rating keeps interest at Rs 21 cr-levels.
3. Acquisition pipeline — every road added from sponsors grows distributable cash flow.

## Red flags
- Lumpy, non-comparable financials: Rs 1,466 cr revenue (FY24) vs Rs 404 cr (FY25) vs TTM Rs 717 cr — hard to read run-rate.
- FY25 net loss of Rs 77 cr despite asset ownership.
- Yield of ~3% looks thin versus other InvITs; growth depends on sponsor drop-downs.

## Verdict
Boring toll-road annuity machine with an AAA stamp — a hold-for-cash asset play, not a growth stock.
