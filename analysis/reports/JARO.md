# Jaro Institute of Technology Management and Research Ltd (JARO)

## What it does
Jaro runs an online higher-education platform selling MBA, DBA, MCA, B.Com and other degree-plus-certification programmes in partnership with universities — including 16 Tier-1 institutions (7 IIMs and 7 IITs among 36 partners). It listed on NSE Emerge in September 2024.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 199 cr (FY24), Rs 252 cr (FY25); TTM Rs 284 cr. 5-yr sales CAGR 45%.
- Operating profit: Rs 63 cr / 32% (FY24), Rs 83 cr / 33% (FY25); TTM OPM softer at 26%.
- PAT: Rs 41 cr (FY24), Rs 52 cr (FY25), TTM Rs 57 cr; 5-yr profit CAGR 71.5%.
- Nearly debt-free (Rs 8 cr borrowings); ROCE 22.1%, ROE 16.5%; P/E ~18.5 at Rs 470/share.

## Lynch lens
A fast grower riding the boring-but-big online-upskilling wave — asset-light, 32%+ margins, near-zero debt. The niche: exclusive-feeling tie-ups with IIMs/IITs that rivals can't easily copy. P/E ~18.5 against 20%+ profit growth gives a PEG under 1 — attractive if growth holds. Caveat: edtech fashions change fast and student enrolment is discretionary spend.

## TAM
India online higher-education and upskilling market — a multi-billion-dollar opportunity as working professionals seek flexible degrees; exact size data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Enrolments in online MBA/executive programmes (core revenue; exact mix data not found).
2. Brand pull of IIM/IIT partnerships supporting premium pricing (33% OPM).
3. Asset-light model — no campus costs, advertising and technology form the main expense base.

## Red flags
- TTM OPM slipped to 26% — rising ad/marketing spend to sustain enrolments.
- Revenue depends on continuation of university partnerships.
- SME-listed with modest market cap (Rs 1,047 cr) and low liquidity.

## Verdict
Asset-light fast grower at a reasonable P/E — promising, if enrolment growth survives the edtech hype cycle.
