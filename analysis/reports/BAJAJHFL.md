# Bajaj Housing Finance (BAJAJHFL)

## What it does
The company lends money for homes. It gives mortgages to buy or renovate houses and commercial property, loans against property, lease-rental loans and construction loans to developers. Bajaj Finance owns it fully, and it ranks as India's second-largest housing finance company with an AUM of Rs 1,33,412 crore (December 2025).

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 5,665 crore (FY23); Rs 7,617 crore (FY24); Rs 9,554 crore (FY25) — ~30% growth
- PAT: Rs 1,258 crore (FY23); Rs 1,731 crore (FY24); Rs 2,163 crore (FY25) — ~25% growth
- Financing (NIM): 31% (FY23) → 29% (FY24/FY25) as per screener's financing-margin line
- Borrowings Rs 82,072 crore (FY25); operating expenses Rs 759 crore (FY25) — very low opex ratio
- Listed September 2024

## RevenueStreams
- Home mortgages and commercial-property loans — core book (FY25)
- Loans against property and lease-rental discounting (FY25)
- Developer finance (FY25)
- Exact mix %: data not found

## Expenses
- Interest on borrowings — dominant cost, funding an Rs 82,072 crore book (FY25)
- Operating expenses Rs 759 crore (FY25) — under 8% of revenue, a cost moat
- Credit provisions: data not found

## Lynch lens
Fast grower with a stalwart parent — 25-30% growth in a boring, understood product (home loans), plus the Bajaj brand and distribution. The Lynch angle here: housing finance compounds predictably as long as credit costs stay low and spreads hold. The NIM glide from 31% to 29% (FY23-FY25) bears watching — competition compresses spreads over time.

## TAM
India outstanding housing credit ~Rs 33-35 lakh crore (FY24-FY25, RBI/NHB data); housing shortage and low mortgage penetration (~11-12% of GDP vs ~60-70% in developed markets) leave a long runway.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Loan book growth (~25-30% AUM growth) at stable spreads
2. Cost of funds — borrowing costs vs lending yield spread
3. Credit costs — low NPAs so far; any slippage hits the 20%+ ROE hard

## Red flags
- Thin spreads in a hyper-competitive mortgage market (SBI, HDFC, LIC HF)
- Rate cycle risk — heavy reliance on market borrowings (Rs 82,072 crore, FY25)
- Parent shadow: growth ambitions could strain capital, needing dilution
- Unsecured/LAP book carries higher credit risk than plain vanilla mortgages

## Verdict
A high-quality fast grower in a boring business — the Bajaj tag commands a premium for a reason.
