# Seshaasai Technologies Limited (STYL)

## What it does
Seshaasai Technologies prints and manufactures secure payment products — debit cards, credit cards, metal cards, QR-cheques and preprinted MICR cheque books — for banks across India. It also makes RFID smart cards and tags, and prints insurance policy documents.

## The numbers
- FY25 revenue: ~Rs 1,463 crore (Rs 14,631.5 million); FY24: Rs 1,558 crore; FY23: Rs 1,146 crore — so FY25 revenue actually dipped vs FY24.
- Annual profit ~Rs 269 crore (per Screener; period label FY25). Market cap ~Rs 6,506 crore at one point.
- Market share: 31.9% of India's debit and credit card issuance in FY25 — a top-two Indian card manufacturer.
- Debit cards drove ~91% of recent growth in cards issuance.
- IPO: Rs 813.07 crore book-built issue (fresh Rs 480 crore + OFS), price band Rs 402-423.
- Expense heads detail: data not found; key costs: PVC/plastic and metal card blanks, chips, printing consumables, and freight.
- Recent quarter (Q1 FY26): profit fell ~41.5% QoQ, revenue -15.1% QoQ to ~Rs 311 crore (HDFC Sky).

## Red-flag note: revenue declined FY24 to FY25 even as profit held — margin/mix dependent business.

## Lynch lens
Stalwart with wobble — a quasi-duopoly in boring bank-card printing, a classic toll-booth-on-payments niche. But revenue fell in FY25 and management itself calls FY26 a "year of transformation and transition", diversifying into metal cards, RFID/IoT and fulfilment services. Not a fast grower right now. P/E data not found here.

## TAM
Indian payment-card issuance market — company already holds 31.9% share (FY25); growth comes from card reissuance cycles, metal/premium cards, and RFID/IoT tags rather than overall market expansion.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Debit-card issuance volumes (~91% of card growth) tied to banking penetration.
2. Mix shift to premium metal/high-tech cards lifting realisations.
3. New legs — RFID tags, IoT and deliverables management — offsetting cheque-book decline.

## Red flags
- Revenue declined YoY in FY25 despite profit holding — needs watching.
- Cheque-book demand structurally shrinks as payments digitise.
- Post-listing quarter showed sharp QoQ profit drop (Q1 FY26).
- Banking-customer concentration.

## Verdict
A duopoly printer of the cards in everyone's wallet — decent niche, but growth needs the new businesses to fire.
