# WeWork India Management Ltd (WEWORK)

## What it does
WeWork India rents out ready-to-use offices by the desk, month or year — flexible workspace under the global WeWork brand, which it licenses exclusively for India. Embassy Group controls it. As of June 2025 it ran 68 centres with ~1.14 lakh desks across 7.67 million sq ft.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 1,665 cr (FY24) → Rs 1,949 cr (FY25) → Rs 2,440 cr (FY26).
- PAT: -Rs 136 cr (FY24) → Rs 128 cr (FY25) → Rs 75 cr (FY26) — profit slipped after turning positive.
- OPM ~63-64% (gross of lease costs); heavy depreciation Rs 967 cr and interest Rs 601 cr (FY26).
- Borrowings jumped to Rs 5,550 cr (Mar 2026) from Rs 310 cr (Mar 2025); promoters pledged 35% of their stake.

## RevenueStreams
Workspace memberships and managed-office contracts form nearly all revenue; exact product split (desks vs managed enterprise): data not found.

## Lynch lens
Turnaround in progress — it crossed into profit in FY25 — but FY26 shows the fragility. Real estate with an asset-heavy lease model plus a licensed global brand. The parent WeWork brand's overseas collapse adds narrative risk though India ops stay separate. P/E and insider buying: data not found.

## TAM
India flexible workspace market estimated at tens of millions of sq ft across major cities (industry estimates, mid-2020s). Precise figure: data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Occupancy and desk pricing across the 68 centres.
2. Depreciation and lease interest — Rs 1,568 cr combined (FY26), the swing factor between profit and loss.
3. New centre additions and enterprise managed-office contracts.

## Expenses
Depreciation Rs 967 cr; interest Rs 601 cr; other operating expenses Rs 867 cr (FY26).

## Red flags
- Debt ballooned ~18x in one year (Rs 310 cr → Rs 5,550 cr).
- Promoter pledge of 35%.
- Parent brand's global failures; profit fell in FY26 despite revenue growth.

## Verdict
A risky turnaround — assets and brand give optionality, but the debt load and pledge make this a watch-list stock, not a buy.
