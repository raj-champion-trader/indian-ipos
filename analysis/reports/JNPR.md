# Juniper Green Energy Limited (JNPR)

## What it does
Juniper Green Energy builds and runs large solar, wind, and hybrid power plants. It sells the electricity under long-term contracts mostly to government-backed buyers. Listed August 2026 after a Rs 1,800 crore IPO.

## The numbers
- Revenue: FY24 Rs 424 cr; FY25 Rs 570 cr; FY26 Rs 805 cr (consolidated, per IPO documents).
- PAT: FY24 Rs 40 cr; FY25 Rs 36 cr; FY26 Rs 40 cr.
- EBITDA margin FY26: ~86% (IPP model — fuel-free once built).
- Portfolio 7,898 MW (10,070 MWp) as of May 2025: solar, wind, wind-solar hybrid, FDRE with battery storage.
- Debt/equity FY26: 2.75. IPO money (Rs 683 cr + Rs 729 cr via subsidiaries) goes to debt repayment.

## Lynch lens
Fast grower in a hot sector — the danger zone. Capacity compounds fast (revenue up ~90% FY24→FY26) but PAT stays flat at ~Rs 40 cr because interest costs eat the growth. Returns stay thin (RoNW 1.18% FY26). Peers trade at silly P/Es (Adani Green ~157x, NTPC Green ~148x), so relative cheapness can mean little. Classic capital-hungry renewables story: growth first, profit later.

## TAM
India renewable capacity target of ~500 GW by 2030 drives the pipeline; specific market-size figure for merchant/PPA renewables: data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. New capacity commissioning (each MW adds contracted revenue).
2. Interest cost on project debt — the single biggest profit killer at D/E 2.75.
3. Wind-solar hybrid and FDRE-with-BESS tariffs (higher-priced firm power sales).

## Red flags
Flat PAT despite ~90% revenue growth; heavy leverage; execution risk on a 7.9 GW pipeline; government-counterparty payment delays; IPO proceeds largely to repay debt rather than fund growth.

## Verdict
Betting on the pipeline, not the profits — a fast grower that has yet to prove it can convert megawatts into shareholder rupees.
