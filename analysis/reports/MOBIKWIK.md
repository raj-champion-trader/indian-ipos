# One Mobikwik Systems (MOBIKWIK)

## What it does
Mobikwik runs a wallet and payments app. Consumers use it to pay bills, recharge phones and pay at shops; the company earns fees from merchants and from arranging small loans (ZIP) through partner banks.

## The numbers
- Revenue: FY21 Rs 289 cr; FY22 527; FY23 539; FY24 875; FY25 1,170 cr (consolidated, screener.in).
- PAT: FY24 +Rs 14 cr; FY25 -Rs 122 cr (EPS -15.64). TTM quarters after FY25 turned positive again (small single-digit profits).
- OPM: FY25 about -9% (FY24 +2.5%); margins swing with payment-processing and marketing costs.
- Borrowings Rs 292 cr (FY24) / Rs 276 cr (FY25); promoter holding ~25%; no dividend.

## Lynch lens
Turnaround attempt inside a fast-growing industry. Payments volume grows 30%+ (5-yr sales CAGR ~31%), but profits bounce between small gains and losses — classic "story stock with no earnings". Payment aggregator licence (2025) gives a niche moat vs UPI freebies. Insider buying: data not found. P/E: data not found (loss-making year).

## TAM
India digital payments market — roughly $3 trillion+ transaction value (FY24, industry reports); credit distribution (BNPL/consumer credit) adds a large layered market. Exact current TAM: data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Payment services fees (gateway, wallet, billpayments) — largest revenue block (~3/4 of revenue, FY24 DRHP mix).
2. Credit monetisation (ZIP take rates and distribution fees).
3. Marketing/processing cost discipline — the swing factor that pushed FY25 into loss.

## Red flags
FY25 loss after FY24 profit; thin, volatile margins; UPI competition compresses payments fees; regulator-driven BNPL changes; reliance on partner lenders.

## Verdict
Speculative turnaround — watch whether aggregator licence and ZIP turn FY26's small quarterly profits into a run-rate.
