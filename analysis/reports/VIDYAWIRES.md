# Vidya Wires Limited (VIDYAWIRES)

## What it does
Vidya Wires makes copper and aluminium winding wires, strips, conductors and busbars — the electrical "veins" inside motors, transformers and EV components. Founded 1981, it makes over 6,400 product types and ranks as India's 4th largest player with 5.7% share of installed capacity (FY25). Customers span power & transmission, electrical systems, automotive, renewables and EVs.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 1,003 crore FY23, Rs 1,182 crore FY24, Rs 1,479 crore FY25 (3-yr CAGR ~22%; TTM growth ~27%).
- PAT: Rs 21 crore FY23, Rs 26 crore FY24, Rs 41 crore FY25; net margin only 2.1%→2.8%.
- OPM 3.7%→4.3% (thin, commodity-pass-through economics); interest Rs 11 crore, depreciation Rs 3 crore FY25.
- Borrowings Rs 141 crore FY25; ROE ~19%, ROCE ~22% FY25.
- SME IPO Dec 2025, subscribed 28.5x.

## Lynch lens
Cyclical with a twist — a copper/aluminium converter with razor-thin margins that lives on metal price spreads and volume. The EV/renewables winding-wire demand angle gives it a growth option, but the P&L behaves like a commodity processor: revenue swings with copper prices, margins stay sliver-thin. Not a niche-dominant player (4th, 5.7% share). Solid 22% ROCE despite 4% OPM shows decent asset turns.

## TAM
Data not found precisely; context — India winding wire market large and fragmented, company at 5.7% capacity share (FY25, RHP).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Copper/aluminium price spread realisation on winding wires (raw material cost pass-through).
2. Volume growth from power/transmission and EV/renewables demand.
3. Finance costs and working-capital efficiency (inventory of metals).

## Red flags
- 2-3% net margins — one bad copper-price cycle wipes profits.
- Heavy working-capital intensity; Rs 141 crore debt vs Rs 41 crore FY25 PAT.
- Fragmented, competitive industry; no pricing power evident.
- Segment-wise revenue split not disclosed in reviewed sources.

## Verdict
A thin-margin commodity converter riding the EV/power theme — cyclical, not a compounder.
