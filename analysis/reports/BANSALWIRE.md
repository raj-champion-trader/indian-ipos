# Bansal Wire Industries Ltd (BANSALWIRE)

## What it does
Draws steel and other metals into wire — stainless steel wire, high-carbon wire, and speciality wires used in cars, fans, fasteners, bearings and hand tools. India's largest stainless-steel wire maker by volume and second-largest wire maker overall.

## The numbers
- Sales: Rs 2,466 cr (FY24) → Rs 3,507 cr (FY25), +42%; TTM Rs 4,389 cr
- PAT: Rs 75 cr (FY24) → Rs 146 cr (FY25), +95%
- OPM: 6% (FY24) → 8% (FY25) → 7% (TTM)
- Interest Rs 60 cr (TTM); Depreciation Rs 60 cr (TTM)
- Borrowings Rs 555 cr (Mar 2026); D/E ~0.41x
- ROCE 13.8%, ROE 12.1%; P/E ~34.5; market cap ~Rs 4,978 cr
- 3-yr sales CAGR 20%, profit CAGR 40%

## Lynch lens
Cyclical in a boring business — wire drawing, the classic Lynch "dull industry" candidate. Leadership in stainless wire gives a niche edge; thin 7-8% operating margins mean raw material (steel rod) pass-through rules profitability. PAT nearly doubled (FY25) on 42% sales growth — strong operating leverage. At 34.5x earnings for a thin-margin cyclical, the price assumes the up-cycle lasts forever; Lynch would prefer buying these at single-digit P/Es in a downturn.

## TAM
India wire-drawing industry estimated at Rs 25,000-30,000 cr, highly fragmented with Bansal a top-2 player (industry estimates; precise figure data not found).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Steel rod prices — raw material dominates costs; spreads decide margins
2. Auto/industrial demand (high-carbon and stainless wire for components)
3. Volume growth plus operating leverage — PAT +95% (FY25) on +42% sales

## Red flags
- Razor-thin 7-8% OPM; commodity spread risk
- P/E 34.5 for a cyclical at likely peak-cycle margins
- Working-capital-heavy business; Rs 555 cr borrowings
- 1-year stock return -11% — momentum cooling

## Verdict
Solid niche leader in a dull industry — good company, wrong price for a cyclical this deep into its up-cycle.
