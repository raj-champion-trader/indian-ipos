# Aye Finance Ltd (AYE)

## What it does
Delhi-based NBFC lending to micro-scale MSMEs — small-ticket business loans (average disbursement about Rs 1.8 lakh) for working capital and expansion. Listed 2025.

## The numbers
- FY26 revenue Rs 1,815 crore vs Rs 1,460 crore FY25 (+24%); PAT Rs 194 crore vs Rs 171 crore (+13%).
- Interest cost Rs 539 crore (FY26) — the single largest expense; operating expenses Rs 1,051 crore.
- Borrowings Rs 5,065 crore; book value Rs 103/share; ROE ~9%.
- 5-year CAGRs: revenue ~30%, profit ~63%.

## Lynch lens
Stalwart-financial — steady mid-20s growth in a focused micro-MSME lending niche (a boring, underserved corner banks avoid). ROE of 9% looks modest vs NBFC peers; the loan book quality cycle decides returns more than growth. No insider buying data found.

## TAM
Indian micro & small enterprise credit gap — a very large, formally unmet market (MSME credit gap estimates run into lakhs of crores; exact figure data not found).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Net interest margin: lending yield minus the Rs 539 crore (FY26) borrowing cost.
2. Asset quality / credit provisions on the micro-MSME book.
3. Operating cost ratio — branch-heavy origination economics (opex Rs 1,051 crore FY26).

## Red flags
- Unsecured-style micro lending carries collection risk in downturns.
- Thin ROE (~9%) against a Rs 5,065 crore leveraged book.
- Interest expense grew as fast as revenue in FY26.

## Verdict
Decent niche lender growing steadily — margin of safety depends on credit discipline, not valuation fireworks.
