# Manba Finance Ltd (MANBA)

## What it does
Manba Finance lends money to buyers of used two-wheelers, three-wheelers, used cars, and to small businesses. It works through 1,118 dealers across 71 locations in 6 states, with the biggest presence in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Chhattisgarh. Rural and semi-urban borrowers who lack formal income proof form the core customer base.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 192 cr (FY24), Rs 250 cr (FY25); interest income Rs 82 cr (FY24), Rs 108 cr (FY25). 5-yr sales CAGR 26%.
- PAT: Rs 31 cr (FY24), Rs 38 cr (FY25). 5-yr profit CAGR 29%.
- Net margin: 16.1% (FY24), 15.2% (FY25). Financing margin ~22%.
- Borrowings: Rs 1,556 cr (FY26); ROE ~12%, GNPA ~3.4% (approx, recent).
- IPO: September 2024 (NSE/BSE SME-era mainboard listing).

## Lynch lens
A small, boring lender in a niche (used two-wheeler financing) that big banks ignore — classic small fast grower territory, growing 26% a year on a tiny base. Asset quality (GNPA ~3.4%) and the high cost of borrowing funds for an small NBFC remain the watch items. P/E data not found; PEG not computable.

## TAM
India used two-wheeler and small commercial vehicle financing market serves tens of millions of rural borrowers underserved by banks; exact market size data not found. Growth tailwind from used-vehicle volumes in tier-3/rural India.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Interest spread: lending yield over cost of borrowings (financing margin ~22%).
2. Used two-wheeler/three-wheeler loan volumes via dealer network expansion.
3. Small business and personal loans added on top of the vehicle book.

## Red flags
- GNPA ~3.4% — small-ticket unsecured-style lending carries default risk.
- Interest cost ~46% of revenue (FY26 TTM): margin lives and dies on cost of funds.
- Small scale; competition from larger used-vehicle NBFCs (Shriram, Chola, Hero FinCorp).

## Verdict
A tiny fast grower in a real niche — watchable only if asset quality stays under control.
