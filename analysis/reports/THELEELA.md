# Leela Palaces Hotels & Resorts Ltd (THELEELA)

## What it does
The Leela runs luxury hotels across India under its famous brand, owning 14 hotels (as of FY26) while also managing hotels for other owners — a mix of owning and managing. Guests pay for rooms, food and banquets at the top end of Indian hospitality.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 1,171 cr (FY24) → Rs 1,301 cr (FY25) → Rs 1,527 cr (FY26).
- PAT: -Rs 2 cr (FY24) → Rs 48 cr (FY25) → Rs 403 cr (FY26) — a dramatic swing to profit.
- OPM 46-48% across FY24-FY26.
- Debt cut from Rs 4,453 cr (FY24) to Rs 1,811 cr (FY26); interest fell from Rs 459 cr (FY25) to Rs 204 cr (FY26).
- Market cap ~Rs 18,543 cr (2026); ROCE ~8.7%.
- Promoters pledged 73.6% of their holding.

## RevenueStreams
Owned-hotel operations (rooms, food & beverage, banquets) form the core; management/franchise fees from asset-light contracts add the balance. Exact split: data not found.

## Lynch lens
Classic turnaround — losses in FY24, Rs 403 cr profit in FY26, driven by luxury-travel boom plus deleveraging after the 2025 IPO (listed as Schloss Bangalore). The Leela brand itself works as the asset play: a scarce Indian luxury name. ROCE ~8.7% still trails the P&L recovery — capital-heavy owned hotels. Insider buying: data not found.

## TAM
Indian luxury hotel sector riding record tourism and rising room rates; premium-segment TAM data not found in precise terms.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Room rates and occupancy at owned luxury hotels (industry-wide rate boom, FY25-FY26).
2. Interest cost halving (Rs 459 cr → Rs 204 cr) after IPO-driven debt reduction.
3. High-margin banquet/F&B business and new management-fee income.

## Expenses
Hotel operating costs (staff, utilities, consumables) form the bulk of Rs 787 cr total expenses (FY26); depreciation on owned hotels adds heavily — exact line split: data not found.

## Red flags
- Promoter pledge of 73.6% — the biggest wart.
- ROCE ~8.7% despite profit surge; asset-heavy model.
- Luxury demand can deflate fast in a downturn.

## Verdict
A genuine turnaround with a prized luxury brand — the pledge and the cyclical nature of hotels keep it a "buy carefully" stock.
