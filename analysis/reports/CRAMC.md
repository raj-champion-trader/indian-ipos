# Canara Robeco Asset Management Company (CRAMC)

## What it does
The company runs mutual funds and sells investment advice. It earns a fee on every rupee customers park in its schemes. Canara Bank owns 51% and Robeco (Netherlands) owns 49%, and it ranks as India's second-oldest fund house (set up 1993).

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 318 crore (FY24); Rs 404 crore (FY25), up 27%
- PAT: Rs 151 crore (FY24); Rs 191 crore (FY25), up 26%
- OPM: 63% (FY24) to 65% (FY25); ROE (3-yr avg) ~34%
- Almost debt-free; dividend payout 26% (FY25)
- TTM revenue Rs 478 crore, PAT Rs 218 crore; P/E ~23.7x at market cap ~Rs 5,183 crore (as of screener snapshot)
- 5-year sales CAGR ~30%, profit CAGR ~41%

## RevenueStreams
- Mutual fund management fees — dominant share of revenue (FY25)
- Investment advisory fees (FY25)
- Exact scheme-wise (equity vs hybrid vs debt) split: data not found

## Lynch lens
Fast grower in a fee-based, asset-light business — 27% revenue growth, 65% operating margins, ROE above 30%. The AMC model scales without extra capital, a classic Lynch "recurring revenue compounding machine." PEG roughly 23.7/26 growth ≈ 0.9 — reasonable if growth holds. The Canara Bank distribution network plus Robeco's process discipline gives a niche angle in tier-2/3 India flows.

## TAM
India mutual fund AUM crossed ~Rs 70 lakh crore in 2025 (AMFI); industry SIP flows ~Rs 27,000 crore/month (2025). Company AUM: data not found in fetched sources.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Management fees tied to average AUM — equity/hybrid fund performance drives flows
2. SIP/retail inflows through Canara Bank branch network
3. Operating leverage — fixed cost base, so fee growth drops largely to profit

## Red flags
- Revenue depends on market levels — a bear market cuts AUM and fees
- Small vs giants (SBI, HDFC, ICICI Pru AMCs) — scale disadvantage in fund costs
- Fierce fee competition from direct plans and passive funds

## Verdict
Quality fast grower at a fair price — a fee machine worth owning if AUM keeps compounding.
