# Emmvee Photovoltaic Power Limited (EMMVEE)

## What it does
Company makes solar PV modules and solar cells at integrated plants near Bengaluru. It sells to customers in India, Europe (especially Germany) and the US, and plans big capacity expansion. Listed on NSE/BSE in November 2025 via a Rs 2,900 crore fresh-issue IPO.

## The numbers
- FY24 revenue ~Rs 954-956 crore; FY25 revenue ~Rs 2,336-2,360 crore (~2.5x growth).
- FY24 PAT ~Rs 9-29 crore (sources differ: ~Rs 28.9 crore per DRHP coverage); FY25 PAT ~Rs 369 crore (~13x jump).
- H1 FY26 (Apr-Sep 2025): revenue Rs 2,158.8 crore, up 193% YoY.
- Capacity (June 2025): 7.80 GW modules, 2.94 GW cells; plans to reach 16.30 GW modules by FY2028 with ~Rs 5,500 crore capex.
- Expense heads: raw material costs (polysilicon, cells, glass) dominate; exact percentages data not found.

## Lynch lens
A fast grower riding India's solar buildout plus exports — revenue and profit multiplied in FY25. But solar module manufacturing sits in a commodity, capital-hungry industry with Chinese price competition and thin terminal differentiation, so it also carries cyclical/commodity traits. Lynch would want the growth to persist several years and debt under control post-IPO (IPO proceeds reduce debt). P/E: data not found; PEG not computable.

## TAM
India solar module demand tied to ~500 GW renewable target by 2030; global solar module market in tens of billions of dollars (exact TAM figure data not found in sources reviewed).

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Solar module sales (~60% of order book per NSE listing note) — volume-driven, boosted by new capacity.
2. Solar cell sales — higher-value integrated output from 2.94 GW cell lines.
3. Exports to US/Europe — priced above domestic market, supporting FY25 margin jump.

## Red flags
- Commodity industry with brutal Chinese competition and falling module prices.
- Heavy capex (~Rs 5,500 crore planned) — debt and dilution risk.
- FY24 base profit was tiny, so the 13x jump flatters optics.

## Verdict
Fast grower in a capital-guzzling commodity industry — ride only with strict price discipline.
