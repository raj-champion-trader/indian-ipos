# Nephrocare Health Services Ltd (NEPHROPLUS)

## What it does
Nephrocare runs India's largest dialysis network under the NephroPlus brand — haemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis and home dialysis through standalone centres, hospital-based captive clinics and government PPP units. Patients return several times a week, giving repeat revenue.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 630 crore FY25 vs Rs 544 crore FY24 (+16%).
- PAT: Rs -8 crore FY25 vs Rs 34 crore FY24 — slipped to a loss. OPM 14% FY25 (17% FY24).
- Interest Rs 50 crore and depreciation Rs 58 crore FY25 — heavy D&A from clinic buildout.
- Borrowings cut to Rs 12 crore FY26 from Rs 133 crore FY25 (post-IPO deleveraging).

## Lynch lens
A potential fast grower in a boring, sticky service — dialysis repeats for life, an annuity-like niche. But expansion costs outran profits in FY25 (turnaround-in-progress). If PPP contracts keep compounding and debt stays low post-IPO, this fits Lynch's "growing in a dull industry" pattern. Watch margin recovery before buying.

## TAM
India dialysis market: commonly estimated at roughly Rs 25,000-35,000 crore with severe under-treatment — India needs far more dialysis sessions than it currently provides (industry/Journal estimates); exact current TAM data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. PPP government dialysis contracts (volume-driven, lower rate but steady).
2. Private-pay standalone centres (higher realisation per session).
3. Clinic-level occupancy vs fixed costs of new centre ramp-up plus interest and depreciation.

## Red flags
- FY25 net loss after FY24 profit; OPM compression.
- Receivables risk on government PPP dues.
- Earlier leverage; interest used to eat a big chunk of operating profit.

## Verdict
Sticky recurring-revenue dialysis network, temporarily loss-making — a possible Lynch fast grower once margins recover.
