# Aadhar Housing Finance Ltd (AADHARHFC)

## What it does
Aadhar Housing Finance gives home loans to low-income families — average ticket size Rs 10 lakh, loans mostly under Rs 15 lakh, loan-to-value ~58.3%. It serves borrowers in smaller towns whom big banks skip. Borrowers repay via EMIs; Aadhar funds itself via bank lines and market borrowing.

## The numbers
- Revenue: Rs 2,043 crore (FY23), Rs 2,587 crore (FY24), Rs 3,109 crore (FY25) — screener.in
- PAT: Rs 545 crore (FY23), Rs 750 crore (FY24), Rs 912 crore (FY25); TTM Rs 1,141 crore
- Financing margin: 36% (FY23), 38% (FY24), 39% (FY25, TTM)
- Interest expense: Rs 799 crore (FY23), Rs 987 crore (FY24), Rs 1,174 crore (FY25) — largest cost
- Other expenses: Rs 507 crore (FY23) → Rs 736 crore (FY25); depreciation Rs 25 crore (FY25)
- ROE 16-18% FY23-FY25; low-LTV (58.3%) secured model

## Lynch lens
A fast grower in a boring, underserved niche — affordable housing credit for self-employed/low-income borrowers outside bank scoring systems. Growth ~22% revenue CAGR with steady 17% ROE and conservative 58% LTV makes it a disciplined compounder rather than a hype story. Government housing-push tailwind helps. P/E data not confirmed; spread over borrowing cost (Rs 1,174 crore FY25 interest) decides everything.

## TAM
India affordable housing finance TAM runs into lakhs of crore (credit gap in low-income segment); exact verified figure and period: data not found.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Home-loan book growth in low-income/small-town segments — core interest income engine
2. Net interest margin discipline (39% financing margin FY25) vs rising borrowing costs
3. Credit costs — 58.3% LTV collateral cushion keeps losses contained

## Red flags
- Interest expense climbing every year (Rs 799 → Rs 1,174 crore FY23-FY25)
- Affordable-segment borrowers feel job/income shocks first in a slowdown
- Competition from specialised HFCs and microfinance players compresses yields
- Regulatory caps on lending spreads could bite

## Verdict
A steady affordable-housing compounder with a collateral cushion — hold through cycles, watch credit costs.
