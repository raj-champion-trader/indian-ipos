# Leap India Limited (LEAPIND)

## What it does
Leap India rents out wooden pallets, crates, and forklifts to factories and logistics companies, which use them and return them instead of buying their own. One pool of assets gets shared across 1,000-plus customers such as Coca-Cola bottlers, Marico, and Daikin.

## The numbers
- Revenue: FY24 Rs 372 cr; FY25 Rs 485 cr; FY26 Rs 747 cr (per IPO documents).
- PAT: FY24 Rs 37 cr; FY25 Rs 38 cr; FY26 Rs 62 cr.
- OPM ~60-61% (Screener standalone) — but interest (Rs 58 cr FY25) and depreciation (Rs 140 cr FY25) sit below the line.
- Borrowings: Rs 676 cr (FY24) → Rs 1,155 cr (FY25).
- Debtor days 174 (FY25); ROCE ~7%; IPO fresh issue Rs 480 cr with Rs 360 cr for debt repayment.

## Lynch lens
Turnaround-flavored asset play. The pooling idea — shared pallets, subscription economics — carries genuine niche appeal, and revenue compounds >20%. But the model eats assets: depreciation and interest swallow most of the 60% operating margin, leaving 6-7% returns on capital and a 174-day receivable cycle. FY26 PAT finally inflected (Rs 62 cr). This one works only if asset turns improve; otherwise it grows revenue and starves shareholders.

## TAM
India pallet-pooling / returnable-packaging market size: data not found in sources fetched.

## Top 3 P/L drivers
1. Asset utilisation rate (pallet turns per year across the pool).
2. Depreciation and interest on the growing leased asset base (Rs 1,155 cr debt FY25).
3. Customer adds in FMCG, 3PL, and quick commerce (1,000+ customers incl. Hindustan Coca-Cola, Marico, Panasonic).

## Red flags
174 debtor days; ROCE ~7% well below cost of capital; debt up 71% in FY25; profit growth far behind revenue growth; asset-heavy model with residual-value risk on wooden pallets.

## Verdict
A clever sharing-economy niche still proving it can earn its cost of capital — watchful buy at best.
